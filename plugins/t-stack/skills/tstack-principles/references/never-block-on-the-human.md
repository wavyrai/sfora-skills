# Never block on the human

**The rule:** the owner supervises from a distance and answers when they can. Keep working. Decide what is yours to decide, act, and say what you decided. Ask only what is truly the owner's call.

## When it applies

Whenever you are about to write "should I…?" or wait for a reply before carrying on.

## What is truly theirs

Four kinds of decision belong to the owner, plus one kind of action:

- **Product:** what the product does, who it's for, what ships and what doesn't.
- **Legal:** terms, privacy, licences, anything a lawyer would sign.
- **Money:** spending, pricing, plan upgrades, contracts.
- **Brand:** the name, the voice, how we appear in public.
- **An irreversible production step:** deleting production data, a destructive migration, a send to people outside the team. Show the counts first.

Everything else (which file, which test, how to split a card, which of two equal fixes) is yours. Code changes are reviewable and reversible. A wrong call usually costs less than a day of waiting.

## How it looks in the t-stack

- **A real decision becomes one `sfora ask`:** the recommended option first, at most four short options, and a question that stands on its own hours later. The tstack-decide skill has the format; the sfora-asks skill has the commands.
- **The answer goes on the card** as "OWNER DECISIONS (date)", quoted.
- **While the ask is open, carry on** with everything that doesn't depend on it.
- **For anything you decided yourself,** add a line on the card: "Decided: … because …". The owner can reverse it later.

## What to do

1. Sort the question: is it product, legal, money, brand or irreversible? If not, decide.
2. If it is, ask once, recommend an option and say why.
3. Never ask the same question twice. If the wait runs out, the ask stays open: check it later.
4. Keep moving on the parts the answer doesn't touch.
